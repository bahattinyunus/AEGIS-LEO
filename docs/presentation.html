⚠️ Why Static Ground-Based Conjunction Warnings Fail in Solar Storms

Standard LEO operations depend on static ground radar Conjunction Data Messages (CDMs) that become obsolete within hours during geomagnetic storms.

1. Thermospheric Expansion (300%–800% Drag Surge)

  • February 2022 Starlink Event: 38 of 49 newly launched satellites were lost when a geomagnetic storm unexpectedly increased atmospheric density by ~50%.
  • May 2024 G5 Superstorm: Extreme solar heating expanded the thermosphere 6x–8x, causing LEO satellites to drift +15 to +26 km along-track and invalidating all static collision predictions.

2. The Ground Blackout Blind Spot (PCA)

  • During severe storms, Polar Cap Absorption (PCA) and ionospheric scintillation sever RF telecommand links with ground stations.
  • When ground operators are blind and unable to uplink commands, the spacecraft must sense thermospheric drag and execute autonomous collision avoidance onboard.
Key Insight: Orbital safety cannot rely solely on ground-in-the-loop CDMs during solar storms; physics-informed intelligence must live onboard the spacecraft flight computer.
